\begin{abstract}
Mapping unknown space does not necessarily reveal where another robot can
complete a manipulation task. We investigate this mismatch in aerial-ground
cooperative retrieval. Our framework projects validated inverse-reachability
candidates through the ground robot's exact footprints, identifying environmental
evidence relevant to future manipulation. This support weights an observation-deficit
field within a finite-window LiDAR acquisition model for aerial viewpoint selection.
Measured ground support and collision-aware screening connect the sensing objective
to physical execution; predicted observations do not substitute for measurements.
We evaluated the closed loop in physics-based simulation with P450, BUNKER,
AUBO i5 and AG95, using 96 independent scenes and 192 primary paired trials.
Under a shared three-window budget, manipulation-aware selection achieved physical
retrieval in 80/96 scenes, compared with 56/96 for Generic NBV.
The policies shared their candidates, sensing model, costs and execution pipeline,
differing only in observation-gain weighting. The paired difference was 25.00
percentage points (prespecified conservative 95\% confidence interval, 8.56--39.15;
exact McNemar $p=8.43\times10^{-6}$). These findings support using downstream
manipulation requirements to allocate aerial sensing in static, known-object
retrieval. General time savings and real-robot performance remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}
